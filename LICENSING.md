# Licensing boundaries

[English](LICENSING.md) | [Português do Brasil](LICENSING.pt-BR.md) | [Português de Portugal](LICENSING.pt-PT.md)

ARA uses separate legal regimes for software, documentation, educational content, research data and project identity.

## Source code

Original source code is licensed under **GNU Affero General Public License version 3 or later** (`AGPL-3.0-or-later`). The repository `LICENSE` file contains version 3 of the license. Source files will use the following SPDX identifier where appropriate:

```text
SPDX-License-Identifier: AGPL-3.0-or-later
```

Third-party components retain their own licenses and must be recorded before adoption.

## Documentation

Original project documentation is licensed under **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International** (`CC BY 4.0`), except where a file states otherwise. See [LICENSE-DOCUMENTATION.md](LICENSE-DOCUMENTATION.md).

## Educational content

The platform will support explicit licensing for each course or educational resource. Official open educational resources may use `CC BY-SA 4.0`, but that license is not imposed automatically on user-created or institutionally restricted content.

## Research data

Participant data, event records, responses, interviews and analytics are not open-licensed by default. Their use and disclosure depend on the applicable research protocol, consent, ethics review, data-protection law and institutional agreement.

## Third-party and restricted material

Articles, books, assessment instruments, datasets, images, institutional documents and other third-party works retain their original terms. Their presence in research notes or citations does not relicense them.

## Project identity

The ARA name, the expansion “Learning Resources Environment” / “Ambiente de Recursos de Aprendizagem”, the kanji, logo and visual identity are not licensed under AGPL or CC BY. See [TRADEMARKS.md](TRADEMARKS.md).

## Contributions

Contributors must have the right to submit their work and must disclose third-party dependencies, sources and license constraints. A contribution must not introduce terms incompatible with the intended distribution of the affected component.
